Section 02 // Signals

Signals, not guarantees.

Automated checks read what the chain can show: pool depth, who holds the supply, which authorities are still live. They flag things to look at. They never mark a token as safe.

Mint address · example Solscan ↗ Solana Explorer ↗ Fictional example address (ends in “demo”) — explorer links show the pattern but will not resolve to a real token.

    Clear means nothing unusual showed up in that one check. Watch means it deserves a closer read. Flag means the chain shows something that commonly precedes losses. None of these is a verdict.

    What checks can’t see

    • Wallets that look independent but are run by one person.
    • Off-chain promises, fake partnerships, paid hype.
    • Token-2022 extensions like transfer fees or hooks need a manual read.
    • Anything that changes after the snapshot — holders sell, pools move.
    • Clear signals can’t stop a token from going to zero.

    Section 04 // How it works

    Four reads, one habit.

    Reach starts from the chain, not the chatter. The loop is the same for every token: find it, read it, compare it, keep an eye on it.

    1. 01

      Discover

      New pairs, trending boards and movers in one sortable view. Search by name, $ticker or the full mint.

      > board --tab new
✓ 8 pairs under 24h
  sorted by age ↑
      →
    2. 02

      Inspect

      Open the mint and read the signals: liquidity, holder spread, mint and freeze authority, LP status, pair age.

      > scan $BFROG
4 clear · 3 watch · 0 flag
  not a verdict
      →
    3. 03

      Compare

      Line it up against its neighbours: volume vs. depth, cap vs. pool, how fast it moved and who is still holding.

      > compare BFROG RAMEN
liq/mcap 11.8% · 12.9%
  top10 41% · 44%
      →
    4. 04

      Watch

      Star it. Your watchlist keeps the shortlist in view so you can come back to it later.

      > watch $BFROG
★ added · 3 watching
  session only
